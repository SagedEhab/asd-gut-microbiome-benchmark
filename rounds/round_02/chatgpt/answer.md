# Round 02 answer (research use; 304 words)

**Can a child's gut-microbial profile predict whether a sampled child belongs to the ASD or control group?** In these three public 16S datasets, **not reliably across cohorts**. A prespecified genus-abundance classifier (family+genus harmonisation, CLR-style transform, L2 logistic regression C=0.01) achieves leave-one-cohort-out AUROC **0.470** on Ding, **0.573** on Zou and **0.551** on David. These estimates are neither consistently above chance nor compelling against 200 cohort-constrained training-label permutations. They should not be interpreted as individual-level clinical performance.

The exploratory network model yields AUROC **0.618** on David (nested-selected C=0.001; raw permutation p=0.010), but across nine principal model/cohort comparisons **Holm p=0.090**. David's matched-sibling concordance is **33/53 (62.3%)** for the network score, modest and uncertain. Within David, complete-case sibling-grouped AUROC is **0.652** using sex/age, versus **0.439** for microbiome abundance and **0.486** for abundance plus sex/age. The metadata contrast is provisional because of disputed control sex coding; nevertheless, the microbiome has not shown incremental value in this protocol.

Zou is especially problematic. Controls have much deeper sequencing; reversing read depth itself yields label AUROC approximately **0.93**. A group-aware cohort-identity model distinguishes Zou from Ding/David at AUROC **0.993**, showing large joint domain shift without identifying its cause. The network result on Zou changes from **0.774** with genus-name-only harmonisation to **0.655** with family+genus harmonisation; its permutation p is **0.398** in the latter analysis. Neither signal can be confidently attributed to ASD rather than assay, depth or cohort structure. Depth matching remains imperfect on Zou despite near-all-subject matching, and fixed-depth rarefaction does not establish independence from batch effects.

Primary literature confirms associations, not an independently locked transferable genus-level classifier; dietary mediation and high meta-analysis heterogeneity further limit inference (Yap et al., DOI:10.1016/j.cell.2021.10.015; Xu et al., DOI:10.3389/fpsyt.2019.00473; Iglesias-Vázquez et al., DOI:10.3390/nu12030792).

**What would change the conclusion?** A genuinely untouched new cohort, a frozen model with reproducible feature generation, AUROC meaningfully greater than 0.5 with uncertainty excluding chance, corrected permutation evidence, depth/batch robustness and added value over available covariates. **Round 3** should prioritise audited sequencing-run overlap, sample-level metadata, batch-balanced validation and a prospectively locked blinded holdout rather than further tuning on these three cohorts.
